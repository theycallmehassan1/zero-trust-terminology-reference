Hajj vs Umrah: Key Differences Explained

Both are pilgrimages to Makkah, but they differ in obligation, timing, length and rites. Here is a clear side-by-side comparison.

Home Blog Hajj vs Umrah

In short: The main difference between Hajj and Umrah is that Hajj is an obligatory pillar of Islam performed only on fixed dates in Dhul Hijjah over five to six days, while Umrah is a highly recommended act that can be done any time of year in a few hours. Both include ihram, Tawaf and Sa’i; only Hajj adds Arafah, Muzdalifah, Mina and the stoning of the Jamarat.

Umrah is often called the “minor pilgrimage” and Hajj the “major pilgrimage.” Umrah can be done any day of the year in a few hours; Hajj happens once a year over five to six days and is one of the five pillars of Islam.

What do Hajj and Umrah have in common?

Both begin with ihram from the miqat, both include Tawaf (seven circuits of the Kaaba) and Sa’i (seven laps between Safa and Marwah), and both end the state of ihram with shaving or trimming the hair. The same ihram prohibitions apply to each.

What does Hajj include that Umrah does not?

Hajj adds the days at Mina, the standing at Arafah, the night at Muzdalifah, stoning the Jamarat over the days of Tashreeq, and the animal sacrifice on Eid al-Adha. These are why Hajj takes several days and requires far more logistics and cost.

Can you do Hajj and Umrah together?

Yes. Most pilgrims travelling for Hajj perform Hajj at-Tamattu’ — an Umrah on arrival, then Hajj a few days later in a fresh ihram. See our Hajj 2027 step-by-step guide for how that works day by day.

Which is more important, Hajj or Umrah?

Hajj is more important: it is one of the five pillars of Islam and obligatory once in a lifetime for every able Muslim. Umrah is highly recommended (sunnah) but not obligatory, and it can be repeated as often as you wish.
